# 🛍️ Nova Retail | Customer Behavior & Business Insights

## Project Overview

This project analyzes customer behavior at **Nova Retail** to identify relationships between purchasing activity, customer-generated revenue, targeted advertising, website visits, Premium membership, and customer churn.

The goal is to transform customer data into actionable business insights while applying appropriate statistical methods for different variable types.

## Business Problem

Nova Retail wants to better understand:

- What behaviors are associated with higher customer-generated revenue?
- How is targeted advertising associated with customer traffic?
- Does Premium membership relate to purchasing behavior or churn?
- Which customer characteristics may be useful for segmentation and retention strategies?

## Dataset

The dataset contains **15,000 customer records and 12 variables**, including demographic, behavioral, marketing, and business-performance information.

Key variables include:

- Monthly visits and purchases
- Targeted advertising spend
- Customer satisfaction
- Premium membership
- Customer churn
- Device type and region
- Customer income level
- Annual revenue generated by each customer

## Key Findings

### 1. Purchase frequency is strongly associated with customer-generated revenue

Monthly purchases and annual revenue generated per customer showed a very strong positive association:

- **Pearson:** 0.9671
- **Spearman:** 0.9675

Customers who purchase more frequently tend to generate substantially more revenue for Nova Retail.

### 2. Targeted advertising is associated with customer visits

Targeted advertising spend and monthly visits showed a moderate positive association:

- **Pearson:** 0.5789
- **Spearman:** 0.5593

Higher advertising investment is associated with greater customer traffic, although the analysis does not establish causality.

### 3. Premium membership alone does not explain purchasing behavior

The point-biserial correlation between Premium membership and monthly purchases was approximately **0.0034**, indicating virtually no association.

### 4. Satisfaction alone does not explain customer churn

The point-biserial correlation between satisfaction and churn was approximately **-0.0238**, suggesting that satisfaction alone is not a strong indicator of customer abandonment.

### 5. Premium membership has only a weak association with churn

Cramér's V between Premium membership and churn was approximately **0.1202**, indicating a weak association.

## Business Insights

The analysis highlights a potential customer journey:

**Targeted Advertising → Visits → Purchases → Revenue**

However, correlation does not establish that one stage directly causes the next.

Nova Retail could prioritize further analysis of conversion and purchase frequency while evaluating whether advertising-driven traffic ultimately produces additional revenue.

Premium membership and satisfaction should not be used independently as strong predictors of purchasing behavior or churn.

## Recommendations

- Identify low- and medium-frequency customers with potential to increase purchase frequency.
- Evaluate conversion from visits to purchases.
- Measure advertising effectiveness using conversion and ROI when campaign-level data is available.
- Analyze high-value customers to identify behavioral patterns that may support retention strategies.
- Explore multivariable churn analysis rather than relying on individual indicators such as satisfaction or Premium membership.
- Compare customer behavior across regions and device types.

## Limitations

- Correlation does not imply causation.
- Several relationships identified are weak or moderate.
- The analysis is limited to the variables available in the dataset.
- Potential outliers may influence some statistical measures.
- Additional temporal and campaign-level data would be needed to evaluate advertising effectiveness more precisely.
